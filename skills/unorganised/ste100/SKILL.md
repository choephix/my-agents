Write the complete answer in ASD-STE100 Simplified Technical English (STE). Preserve the technical meaning. Do not remove necessary warnings, conditions, or limits to make the text shorter.

## Controlled vocabulary

- Use a simple, approved word for its approved meaning when you know it.
- Use one term for one object, action, or idea. Do not use synonyms for variety.
- Use technical names and project terms when an approved general word cannot express the exact meaning.
- Do not invent an STE approval claim. If exact conformance is important and the official dictionary is not available, say that the text follows STE writing rules but is not dictionary-verified.

## Grammar and sentences

- Write in active voice where possible. Name the person, system, or component that does the action.
- Use the imperative for instructions: `Close the valve.`
- Write one instruction in each sentence.
- Keep procedural sentences at 20 words or fewer. Keep descriptive sentences at 25 words or fewer.
- Keep noun clusters to three words or fewer. Add prepositions or split the phrase when necessary.
- Use complete sentences. Do not omit articles, subjects, or verbs only to reduce the word count.
- Put a condition before the action: `If the light is red, stop the test.`
- Use short paragraphs. Keep one topic in each paragraph.

## Clarity

- State the result or required action first.
- Make references explicit. Replace unclear words such as `it`, `this`, and `that` when they can refer to more than one thing.
- Use lists for multiple actions, conditions, or items.
- Do not use idioms, humor, rhetorical questions, or decorative language.
- Explain abbreviations at first use unless the reader must already know them.
- Use consistent units, labels, capitalization, and punctuation.

Before you send the answer, check every sentence for one meaning, one stable term, active construction, and the applicable word limit. The answer is complete when all technical meaning remains and each sentence passes this check.
